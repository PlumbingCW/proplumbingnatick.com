Plan the install before the old tank quits

Most water heaters give warning signs for months. A little rust at the fittings, a warmer-than-usual utility room or a heater past its twelfth year are all reasons to plan. Installing on your schedule means you pick the unit, not whatever is on a truck.

If your tank is already leaking, skip ahead to our emergency water heater replacement page. If it is only aging, this page walks through what a good installation involves in a Natick house.

How an installation is planned

A good install begins before anyone touches a wrench.

1

Size the household.

We ask how many people live in the house and how many bathrooms run at once, then match the unit to real demand.

2

Check fuel and venting.

Gas, electric and heat pump units each need something different from the space around them.

3

Check the space.

Clearances, floor drains, access routes and a way to carry out the old unit are all verified.

4

Confirm code items.

Expansion tank, relief valve discharge, shutoffs and seismic or strap requirements are noted in the plan.

5

Set the day.

You get a start window and a clear price before the day arrives.

Unit types compared

Each type suits a different Natick home.

TypeBest fitWatch for
Gas tank.Homes with an existing gas line and flue.Venting condition and combustion air.
Electric tank.Homes without gas, or small households.Electrical capacity and slower recovery.
Tankless gas.Larger households that want endless hot water.Gas line size, venting and yearly flushing.
Heat pump tank.Efficiency-minded households with open space.Room volume, condensate drain and cooler air near the unit.

A technician confirms fit on site. Heat pump units may appear in Mass Save's related rebates, so check the current program before you buy.

What Natick conditions mean for an install

Natick's 2025 water report lists hardness around 108 parts per million, so a new tank benefits from an anode rod check every few years and a yearly flush. Tankless units benefit even more from descaling, which we cover on our tankless water heater page.

Housing age matters too. With about 45 percent of Natick homes built in 1959 or earlier, many basements still have old gas lines, undersized vents or shutoffs that no longer close. We replace those pieces during the install instead of connecting a new heater to tired parts.

What a complete install includes

Ask any plumber for this list and compare.

  • New water and gas or electrical connections, tested for leaks.
  • A new expansion tank where the system needs one.
  • A relief valve with a properly routed discharge line.
  • New shutoff valves on the supply lines.
  • Removal of the old unit and a clean work area.
  • A walkthrough of temperature setting, shutoffs and maintenance.

What shapes an installation price

The unit is only part of the job.

Type and capacity. Tankless and heat pump units cost more than a standard tank.
Fuel work. Adding a gas line or upgrading electrical changes the scope.
Venting. Replacing a flue or moving to power venting adds parts.
Access. Narrow stairs and attic installs take more time.
Permits. Ask the Natick building department whether your job needs one and we will line up the paperwork.

Neighborhoods and what we usually find

Around Natick Center and South Natick, we often find older cast iron drains and original gas piping near the heater. In newer subdivisions toward West Natick, vents tend to be modern but can be undersized for a tankless swap. Condos near Natick Mall and Route 9 may have shared venting or space limits, which is why we look before we quote.

If your heater is in a finished basement near Lake Cochituate, we also check for moisture and a floor drain, since a leak there can go unnoticed.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

New installations follow 248 CMR and the unit's published installation standards, including clearances, combustion air and a safe discharge from the relief valve. The Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses the trades that do this work, and homeowners can verify a license on the state lookup.

Sources: 248 CMR, manufacturer installation standards and the state plumbers and gas fitters board.
