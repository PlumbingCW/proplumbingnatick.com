No heat tonight? Check these first

Check the thermostat is set to heat and that its batteries are fresh. Look at the boiler pressure gauge, which should sit in the normal range marked on the dial. Make sure the power switch is on and the fuel supply is open. If you smell gas or see soot, shut the boiler off and leave the house.

If your pipes are at risk of freezing, keep warm and open cabinet doors on exterior walls. We take boiler calls overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available. Do not use a stove or oven to heat the house.

Winter in Natick and the boiler

Many Natick homes, especially the older ones around Natick Center, South Natick and Wethersfield, heat with hot water or steam boilers. They run hard from late fall through March. A boiler with a weak part fails exactly when the temperature drops, so an annual tune-up before heating season is worth the money.

When a boiler stops in a deep freeze, pipes in exterior walls can freeze fast. That is why speed matters, and why we discuss burst pipe risk on the same call.

Repair or replace the boiler

Age and parts availability decide it.

Repair

  • Fits a boiler under about fifteen years.
  • Part failures like pumps and valves are routine.
  • Keeps your existing radiators and piping.
  • Costs less today.

Replace.

  • Better for old, leaking or inefficient units.
  • Newer boilers burn fuel more efficiently.
  • Parts for very old boilers can be hard to find.
  • A plan helps you avoid a mid-winter failure.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Boiler repairs are governed by state rules for gas fitting and by manufacturer guidelines. Combustion and venting corrections matter for carbon monoxide safety, so a working CO detector is the minimum standard in any home with a fuel-burning boiler. Ask about permits for replacements.
