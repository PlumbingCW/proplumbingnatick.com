Why Natick plumbing has its own quirks

Natick housing runs old. According to the town's 2025 community profile, about 45 percent of its 15,889 homes were built in 1959 or earlier. Homes from that era often still carry galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains and clay sewer laterals, and each of those fails in a different way.

A rusty-looking drip under a Natick Center kitchen sink and a slow tub in South Natick can both trace back to pipe that has simply reached the end of its life.

The water itself is moderate. Natick's 2025 water quality report lists hardness around 108 parts per million, which works out to roughly 6.3 grains per gallon. That is firm enough to leave scale inside a water heater tank over the years, so older tanks near Lake Cochituate and out toward Walnut Hill often show sediment problems before the tank itself rusts through.

Sewer is a separate story. Natick's sewage is treated by the MWRA, and your sewer charge follows the water meter. That makes a running toilet or an outdoor leak a double cost on the bill. If your meter dial moves while every fixture is off, you likely have a leak, and the town itself tells residents to call a licensed plumber.

Sources: Town of Natick 2025 Consumer Confidence Report, Natick community profile (2025) and the Natick Water and Sewer Division FAQ.
