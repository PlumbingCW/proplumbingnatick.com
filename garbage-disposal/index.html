Disposal jammed or leaking? Do this first

Switch the disposal off at the wall switch and, if you can, unplug it under the sink. Never put your hand inside. If it hums but does not spin, it is jammed, and some models have a hex socket on the bottom that turns the blades by hand. If it leaks, put a bucket under it and shut off the dishwasher.

Call us. We handle disposal emergencies overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available. A leaking disposal can ruin the cabinet floor quickly, so do not wait.

A disposal that leaks from its body usually means replacement.

What to keep out of a disposal

Most clogs start with what goes in.

  • Grease and oils, which coat the pipe walls and harden.
  • Coffee grounds in quantity, which pack into sludge.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and clump.
  • Fibrous items like celery and corn husks.
  • Bones and fruit pits.
  • Anything that is not food.

Disposals and older Natick kitchens

In older houses, the kitchen drain line is often small and has years of grease on its walls. A new disposal does not fix that. If a disposal keeps jamming or the sink drains slowly even after it runs clear, the line downstream likely needs attention, and our drain cleaning team can clear it.

Disposals draw from the same kitchen drain that serves the dishwasher. When one backs up into the other, the problem is downstream. Look at the dishwasher air gap or high loop, since a missing one lets the dishwasher drain into the disposal.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Disposals connect to the kitchen drain and, often, the dishwasher, so the connections follow 248 CMR and the manufacturer's guidelines. The electrical side, such as a new outlet or switch, is a separate trade and needs a licensed electrician where wiring changes.
