How East Natick took shape

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey says a road linking Oak Street and Winter Street to the Boston Post Road existed by the middle of the 1700s. The Boston and Worcester Turnpike later passed through East Natick. After about 1930 strip commercial development spread along Route 9.

The Natick master plan adds that between 1915 and 1940 residential building shifted north toward Route 9 and East Natick, and that growth followed the highway and the postwar boom. The documents describe a time frame, not a house-by-house age. They do not tell us what pipes any home contains.

For you, the useful point is the mix. Homes sit near a busy commercial corridor, so a plumber here often moves between a house on Oak Street and a shop on Route 9 in the same day.

Sources: the 1980 Massachusetts Historical Commission Natick town report, the Natick master plan introduction chapter and the Wikipedia overview of Natick villages.

Find the leak before the bill finds you

Natick bills sewer use from the water meter, so a running toilet or an outdoor leak shows up on both charges. Shut off every fixture, then watch the meter's leak indicator. If it moves, water is going somewhere.

Natick's Water and Sewer Division can tell you how to read your meter. Their number is (508) 647-6557. If the dial still moves with everything off, call us and we will locate the leak.
