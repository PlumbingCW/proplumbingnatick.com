Whole house backing up? Here is the first move

Hold all water use. A full main line means every gallon you add has nowhere to go but up. Find the cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement, and leave the cap on unless a plumber tells you to open it. Open it carelessly and sewage can flood the floor.

Call us now. We handle main line clogs overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available. Tell us how many fixtures are affected and whether a basement floor drain is involved.

What clogs a main line

Cause decides method.

Tree roots.

They enter at joints and spread into dense masses that catch paper.

Grease.

Congealed fats narrow the pipe over years.

Wipes and paper.

Wipes marked flushable rarely break down.

Scale and rust.

Old cast iron builds a rough, narrowing wall.

A belly in the pipe.

A sag holds water and collects solids.

Foreign objects.

Toys and hygiene products reach the main more than people admit.

How we clear a main line

We use the lightest tool that works.

Cable machine

  • Cuts roots and breaks through blockages.
  • Fast, with the pipe wall left rough.
  • Best for a first-time clog.
  • May need repeat visits if buildup remains.

Hydro jetting.

  • Washes the full pipe wall.
  • Best for grease and scale.
  • Pair with a camera for best results.
  • Details on our hydro jetting page.

How Natick houses affect the main

The oldest streets near Natick Center and South Natick have older laterals, and mature trees reach those joints. In newer areas toward West Natick, clogs often come from wipes and grease rather than roots. The first question we ask is how old the house is and how big the trees are.

The sewer charge follows your water meter, and the MWRA treats Natick's sewage. The street main is the town's concern, while the lateral from your house is yours. If water backs up in several homes on your block at once, call the Natick Water and Sewer Division, since the problem may be in the main.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Cleaning follows equipment guidelines rather than code, but opening a line to add a cleanout is plumbing work under 248 CMR. Natick's water and sewer division is the right place to confirm where your responsibility ends and the town main begins.

Sources: equipment guidelines, 248 CMR and the Natick Water and Sewer Division.
