When repiping beats patching

A single leak in a sound system is a repair. Several leaks in a few years, rust-colored water, weak pressure and pinhole sprays all suggest the pipe itself is wearing out. Each patch buys time but leaves the rest of the line to fail on its own schedule.

Repiping replaces the supply lines through the house. It is a bigger job, so we only recommend it when the evidence supports it, and we show you what we found.

Pipe materials and what they mean

Each generation of plumbing ages differently.

MaterialTypical eraCommon issue
Galvanized steel.Early to mid 1900s.Internal rust, low pressure, rusty water.
Copper.1960s onward.Pinholes and corrosion in aggressive water.
Polybutylene.Late 1970s to mid 1990s.Brittle fittings that can fail without warning.
PEX.1990s onward.Fittings and UV exposure, but a strong modern choice.

A plumber confirms your material on site before recommending anything.

Natick housing age and pipes

The town's 2025 community profile shows about 45 percent of Natick homes built in 1959 or earlier and another 16 percent between 1960 and 1979. Homes from the earlier group may still have galvanized lines, and the later group may carry early copper or polybutylene. Our leak detection work often reveals the pattern.

Natick's water report lists lead at the 90th percentile as not detected and copper at 0.36 parts per million, which is good for the water supplied. Your house's own pipes are separate. If you are unsure what material you have, ask us to look.

PEX or copper for the new lines

Both are good choices.

PEX

  • Flexible, so it snakes through walls with fewer openings.
  • Resists freezing splits better than rigid pipe.
  • Generally faster to install.
  • Needs protection from sunlight.

Copper.

  • Long record and durable.
  • Handles heat well.
  • More labor to install.
  • Water chemistry can cause pinholes over time.

What drives repiping cost

Every house is different.

House size. More fixtures and longer runs cost more.
Material. PEX and copper differ in price.
Access. Finished walls and slabs take more time.
Drywall repair. Patching is part of the plan.
Permits. Ask the Natick building department about requirements.
A whole-house number, in writing

Insurance does not usually pay for repiping due to age, though sudden damage from a failure may be covered. After we survey the pipes, you get a written scope and price covering materials, access and patching.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Repiping follows the standards in 248 CMR, including pipe materials approved for potable water and requirements for fittings and testing. Ask the Natick building department about permits, and ask your plumber for the pressure test results when the job is done.
