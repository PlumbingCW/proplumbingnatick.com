Toilet overflowing? Stop it in thirty seconds

Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl. Then close the shutoff valve behind or beside the toilet by turning it clockwise. Do not flush again. Use towels to hold back water and move rugs out of the way.

If water keeps rising or the floor drain is also backing up, the problem is deeper than the toilet and may be in the main line. Call us. We handle toilet emergencies overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available.

Toilet symptoms and what they usually mean

Most problems fall into a few buckets.

SymptomLikely causeFix
Constant running.Worn flapper or fill valve.Replace parts.
Weak flush.Clogged rim jets or a partial clog.Clean jets, clear the trap.
Water at the base.Failed wax ring or loose bolts.Reset the toilet with a new ring.
Phantom refill.Slow tank leak into the bowl.Replace the flapper.
Rocking toilet.Loose flange or bolts.Secure or repair the flange.
Frequent clogs.Low-flow limitations or a downstream blockage.Clear the line, consider a new toilet.

A plunger solves a simple clog. If it does not, stop and call.

Repair or replace the toilet

The age and condition decide it.

Repair

  • Good for a failed flapper, fill valve or seal.
  • Quick and low cost.
  • Keeps a toilet you like.
  • Not worth it on a cracked bowl or tank.

Replace.

  • Better for cracks, chronic clogs or old high-flow models.
  • Newer toilets use less water per flush.
  • Comes with a new wax ring and supply line.
  • Gives you a chance to choose comfort height.

Water use and your Natick bill

Natick bills sewer from water use, so a running toilet shows up twice. A flapper that leaks slowly can waste a lot of water over a month without any sound. If your bill jumped, check the toilets first by dropping food coloring in the tank and seeing whether it reaches the bowl without a flush.

Older homes near Natick Center often still have toilets from decades ago that use far more water per flush than modern models. If yours is one, replacing it can cut water use. For leaks that are not at the toilet, our leak detection service tracks them down.

How a toilet replacement goes

A typical swap is a short visit.

1

Shut off and drain.

We close the supply and empty the tank and bowl.

2

Remove the old toilet.

The old toilet is lifted out and the flange inspected.

3

Check the flange.

A damaged flange is repaired before the new toilet is set.

4

Set the new toilet.

A new wax ring and bolts seat it level.

5

Connect and test.

We connect the supply, flush several times and check for leaks.

What affects toilet repair cost

A new flapper and a new toilet are different jobs.

The failed part. Small parts are quick, flange repair takes longer.
Toilet model. You choose the toilet, and prices vary a great deal.
Floor condition. Soft or rotted subfloor needs repair first.
Access. Tight bathrooms take more time.
Parts or a whole toilet, priced up front

Insurance may help if a toilet overflow damages the floor below, but not the toilet itself. Tell us what the toilet is doing and we will say whether parts or a new fixture is the better value.

Code, permits and licensing for this work
