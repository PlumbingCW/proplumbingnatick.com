What waterproofing means from a plumber's view

A damp basement has only a few sources. Water can seep through the walls and floor from groundwater, run in from a poorly graded yard, come from a leaking pipe or back up through a drain. A plumber is best at the last two and at moving groundwater out with sumps, drains and discharge lines.

We do not seal cracks or build foundations. If your problem is structural, we say so. If it is water the plumbing can solve, our work usually costs less than a full excavation.

Plumbing fixes that dry a basement

These are the jobs we handle.

  • Installing or replacing a sump pump and pit, as in our sump pump service.
  • Adding a battery backup so the pump runs through a power outage.
  • Rerouting discharge lines away from the foundation.
  • Fixing leaking pipes and failed fittings in the ceiling or walls.
  • Clearing the main line that backs up through floor drains.
  • Installing a backwater valve to stop sewer water coming in.

Moisture and flooring

Many Natick homeowners finish a basement and then discover moisture. The fix is not the flooring, it is the water source. Planks and tile over a damp slab trap moisture and grow mold. Fix the leak or the drainage first, then choose flooring that tolerates a damp environment.

Houses near Lake Cochituate and the low ground along the Charles River side of town often have higher water tables, so a sump with a backup is a common and sensible choice. Natick Center homes built decades ago may also have older drain tile that has silted up.

What to do before we arrive

Stay safe while the basement is wet.

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, panels or appliances sit in it.
  • Do not touch a pump or its cord while standing in water.
  • Move valuables and electronics off the floor.
  • Shut the main water valve if a pipe is the source.
  • Photograph the water level and damage for your insurer.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

The plumbing parts of waterproofing, such as drains, sumps and backwater valves, follow the standards in 248 CMR. Structural and exterior drainage work falls to other trades. Ask the Natick building department which permits apply, and get any foundation or exterior work scoped separately.

Sources: 248 CMR and the Natick building department.
