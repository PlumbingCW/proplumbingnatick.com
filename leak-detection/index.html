Suspect a leak? Do the meter test now

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water. Then look at your water meter. Natick's water division says the meter has a leak-detection dial, and if it moves while everything is off, water is escaping somewhere. That is a quick, free check you can do before we arrive.

If water is actively pouring, close the main valve and call. We take leak calls overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available. Keep electronics and outlets clear of wet areas while you wait.

A clue is not a diagnosis. We confirm the source before opening anything.

How we find a leak without tearing things apart

We start with pressure tests that tell us whether the leak is on the supply or the drain side. Then we use acoustic listening gear and moisture meters to narrow the location. Thermal imaging helps on warm lines. The goal is a small, exact opening instead of a torn-up room.

Once we find the source, we explain the repair options and what they involve. For a burst line, our burst pipe repair service covers the fix. For an outside line, see water service line repair.

The bill is a clue

Natick bills sewer use from the water meter, so a leak does double damage on the bill. Summer outdoor use already raises bills because of tiered rates, so compare the same season year over year before concluding you have a leak. A bill that jumps while the family and the lawn habits stay the same is worth a meter test.

A running toilet is the most common silent leak. A faulty flapper can waste a great deal of water without any visible sign. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and see whether color shows up in the bowl without flushing.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Finding a leak follows industry testing standards and does not need a permit, but repairing the pipe is plumbing work under 248 CMR and must be done by a licensed plumber in Massachusetts. Standard practice is a documented pressure test before and after the repair so the result can be shown to you or your insurer.

Sources: 248 CMR and common industry pressure-testing practice.
