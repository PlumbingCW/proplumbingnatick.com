Plan the plumbing before the walls close

The cheapest time to move a drain or add a line is while the walls are open. Plumbing decisions made late cost more because they force demolition. If you are remodeling, bring us in before the drywall goes up.

The code and your project

Massachusetts plumbing work follows the Uniform State Plumbing Code under 248 CMR, and the state licenses plumbers through its Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. You can check any license on the state's lookup tool. In Natick, permits go through the town's Community and Economic Development office, and the building department can tell you whether your job needs one.

We do not guess at permit rules, and we do not quote code from memory. We describe the work to the town and follow its direction.

Choosing fixtures that last

Fixtures are the part you see every day, so pick them with the plumbing in mind. Ask about shutoff valves that can be reached without a ladder, supply lines made of braided steel rather than thin plastic and drains sized for the fixture. A cheap fixture with a cheap connector often costs more over time. We will tell you if a choice will cause trouble before it is installed.
