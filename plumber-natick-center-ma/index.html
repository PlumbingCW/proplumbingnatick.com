What the record says about Natick Center

The railroad reached Natick Center in 1835 and the area grew fast as the town's main village, with shoemaking at its heart. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey records new subdivisions northeast of North Main Street and south of Central Street, and more growth on lots laid out between about 1850 and 1870.

A fire in 1874 burned most of the central business district, and the brick buildings that line downtown today went up afterward. The Natick Center Historic District, on North Avenue, Main, Central and Summer Streets, was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on December 16, 1977. The Massachusetts Cultural Council made the center a state cultural district in 2012.

The practical point is that downtown buildings are a patchwork. A brick block rebuilt after 1874 may hold a restaurant on the ground floor and apartments above, with plumbing added in several decades. Knowing which valve controls which unit is half the job on any call here.

Sources: Wikipedia entry for the Natick Center Historic District, the 1980 Massachusetts Historical Commission Natick town report and the Natick master plan introduction chapter.

Ask the building owner first

If you rent an apartment or a storefront, report a leak to the owner or property manager right away, then call us if you have been told to arrange the repair. Who pays and who may open a wall is set by your lease, not by us.

If you own the building, know where each shutoff leads before you need it. Label the valves for each unit and tell tenants where the main shutoff is. That small step turns a flooded hallway into a short interruption.
