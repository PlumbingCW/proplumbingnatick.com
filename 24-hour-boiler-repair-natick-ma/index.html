When the boiler dies at midnight, you need a calm order of operations. This guide shows what to check, what is safe and when to call.

Boiler triage

Published 2026-11-11. Updated 2026-11-14.

First, safety

If you smell gas, see soot or hear a roaring or hissing from the boiler, stop. Leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. Do not turn lights on or off and do not use a phone near the boiler. Fire-safety guidelines call for working carbon monoxide detectors, and if one alarms, get out and call emergency services.

If none of those apply, you can run through simple checks. Most no-heat calls turn out to be a setting, a power switch or a pressure problem.

Five-minute triage

Work through these in order.

1

Thermostat.

Set to heat, higher than room temperature, with fresh batteries.

2

Power.

Check the boiler switch and the breaker or fuse.

3

Fuel.

Confirm the gas valve is open or the oil tank has fuel.

4

Pressure gauge.

Compare to the range marked on the dial. Very low pressure can shut the boiler down.

5

Reset once.

If the boiler has a reset button, press it one time. Repeated resets can flood an oil burner or hide a problem.

Winter in Natick and what the housing tells us

Natick's 2025 community profile shows 45 percent of its 15,889 homes were built in 1959 or earlier. Many of those older houses around Natick Center, South Natick and Wethersfield were built with hot water or steam heat, and some boilers still run from decades ago. They are tough, but an old boiler can fail without much warning on the coldest night of the year.

That timing matters for pipes. A house left without heat during a hard freeze can see pipes in exterior walls split within hours. If the boiler cannot be fixed fast, protect the pipes first.

Source: Town of Natick community profile (2025).

Protect the house while you wait

Do these if you will be without heat for a while.

  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls so warm air reaches pipes.
  • Let a faucet drip on lines in exposed walls.
  • Close off unused rooms and hang blankets over drafty windows.
  • Use safe space heaters, plugged directly into the wall, never an oven or stove.
  • Know where the main water shutoff is in case a pipe splits.

Triage or call right away

Decide by the temperature and the symptoms.

Triage first

  • The house is still warm and it is not freezing.
  • No gas smell or leak.
  • A thermostat, switch or breaker fix is likely.
  • You can wait for a morning visit.

Call right away.

  • A freeze is forecast and the house is cooling.
  • Pressure is low or water is leaking.
  • You smell gas or hear odd noises.
  • Elderly people, infants or pets are at risk.

Understanding hot water and steam systems

A hot water boiler heats water and pumps it through pipes to radiators or baseboards. A steam boiler heats water into steam that rises through pipes and condenses in radiators. They look similar in a basement but fail in different ways. Steam systems depend on a correct water level and on working vents. Hot water systems depend on pressure and circulation.

Knowing which you have helps you describe the problem on the phone. If you are unsure, look for a pressure gauge and a sight glass. A sight glass showing a water line points toward steam.

Carbon monoxide and a working detector

Any fuel-burning boiler can produce carbon monoxide if it is not burning correctly. A working CO detector on every level and near sleeping areas is a basic safety step. Test it monthly and replace it on schedule.

If an alarm sounds, leave the house and call emergency services from outside. Do not try to find the cause yourself. A plumber or heating technician can check the appliance after the home has been cleared.

What to tell the dispatcher

Give us the basics so the right person comes with the right parts. Say whether the boiler is gas or oil, hot water or steam, and roughly how old it is. Describe what you checked and what you saw on the pressure gauge. Mention any noises, smells or leaks. Tell us whether anyone in the house is elderly, very young or ill, since that changes how fast the visit matters.

A photo of the boiler's label and the control panel is helpful if you can send one. The more we know before we arrive, the more likely the first visit solves the problem.

Plan ahead for next winter

Schedule an annual tune-up before heating season, bleed radiators in the fall and know where the shutoffs are. Replace a failing boiler before it quits at midnight. We serve Needham, Wellesley and nearby towns for these calls, and our line is open around the clock.
