How Framingham's water shapes plumbing calls

Framingham's water comes from the MWRA, with the Carroll treatment plant in Marlborough supplying the city through the MetroWest tunnel. According to MWRA, its water is soft, about 1 grain per gallon, and its pH is kept high, around 9.0 to 9.5, to reduce lead and copper leaching. For you, that means less scale in water heaters than in many nearby towns, but older copper still pits at times.

The city also reports that all known lead service lines were replaced, the last in April 2016. That does not cover the pipes inside your house, so an older home with original galvanized or lead-soldered plumbing is still worth a look. The city's public works office is at 150 Concord St and can answer questions about the street side of your water and sewer.

Sources: MWRA water quality information, City of Framingham water and sewer pages.

The river and the basement

The National Weather Service recorded a record crest of 13.99 feet on the Sudbury River at Saxonville on March 31, 2010, against a flood stage of 10 feet. The earlier crest that month reached 12.92 feet. Homes near the river learned how quickly basements fill when the ground is saturated and storm drains are slow.

The practical step is to test your sump pump before spring and have a battery backup. Framingham's own sewer page lists sump pumps connected to the sewer as a source of inflow, so discharge outdoors and ask the city what is allowed for your address.
