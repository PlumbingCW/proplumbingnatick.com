Business plumbing emergency? Start here

Find the main shutoff and close it if water is spreading. If sewage is backing up, stop using restrooms and sinks. Keep customers and staff away from wet floors, and move inventory off the ground. If a gas smell is present, evacuate and call the gas utility from outside.

Call us. We handle commercial emergencies overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available. Tell us your business type, the address and what you see.

Natick's commercial corridors

Many Natick businesses cluster along Route 9, Speen Street and the area around Natick Mall, plus smaller shops in Natick Center and along Route 135. Older buildings in the center often share old drain stacks, while the large retail strips along Route 9 rely on grease and floor drains that need regular attention.

If your business sits in a building with several tenants, a clog in the shared line may not be inside your space. We check before we charge. For recurring grease problems, hydro jetting is often a better fit than cabling.

Working around your business hours

Most owners want the plumber in when the doors are closed. We plan after-hours work around your closing time, protect floors and fixtures, and leave the space ready to open. If a repair needs a second visit, we schedule it outside your busiest hours rather than during them.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Commercial plumbing follows the standards in 248 CMR plus any rules specific to the use of the building, such as grease interceptors for food service. Landlords and tenants may split responsibility, so ask the building department and your lease about who handles permits and repairs.

Sources: 248 CMR and the Natick building department.
