Who we are

Natick Pro Plumbing is a local plumbing company based at 209 West Central St in Natick, Massachusetts. We take calls around the clock for burst pipes, backed-up drains, failed water heaters and the other problems that cannot wait until Monday.

We work across Natick and the neighboring towns of Framingham, Wellesley, Wayland, Needham and Ashland. Keeping the territory tight means we know the housing, the water and the town offices that matter to your repair.

How we work

Every job follows the same short list of commitments.

  • Clear price before work starts. You hear the number and the scope first, and changes get a conversation before they get a bill.
  • Same-day appointments when available. We tell you honestly on the call whether today is possible.
  • Licensed and insured. Work is done by licensed, insured plumbers, and you can verify a Massachusetts plumbing license on the state lookup.
  • Plain explanations. We tell you what failed, why it failed and what to watch for afterward.

The local work we do most

Natick homes skew old, and that shapes the calls. Many houses around Natick Center, South Natick and Walnut Hill were built decades ago, so we see aging galvanized lines, cast iron drains and clay sewer laterals alongside newer copper and PEX. Water heaters take a beating from moderately hard water, and basements near Lake Cochituate and the Charles River side of town make sump pumps a regular topic.

Commercial calls come from the shops and offices along Route 9, Speen Street and the Natick Mall area, where a failed fixture or a grease-clogged line can close a business for the day. We handle those after hours as well.

Where we work inside Natick

Our crews know the town by village as well as by street. Natick Center, around Natick Common and the Morse Institute Library, has some of the oldest plumbing we see. South Natick, along the Charles River side, brings basements and sump pumps into most conversations. East Natick and West Natick have a mix of mid-century houses and newer builds, and Walnut Hill and Wethersfield have their own older streets.

Knowing which village a call comes from helps us guess what we will find before we get there. A house near Lake Cochituate or Jennings Pond may have moisture issues, while a ranch off Route 27 may have a tank water heater from the 1990s. We use that guess to bring the right parts and to ask better questions on the phone.

What the first phone call is like

You reach a person who asks what you see, hear or smell, where in the house it is and whether water is still running. We then tell you what to shut off and what to leave alone. If it is a true emergency, we start lining up your visit right away. If it can wait a day, we say so and offer a time.

Before work begins, you hear the price and the scope. If the plumber finds something different, we stop and explain the change before going further. That is the whole routine, and it is the same at midnight as it is at noon.

How we think about repair versus replacement

A good plumber saves you money by saying no. If a toilet needs a new flapper, we replace the flapper and leave the toilet. If a water heater has a bad thermostat, we fix the thermostat and let the tank keep running. When a fixture or appliance truly is finished, we show you the cracked tank, the rusted fitting or the failed seal so you can see the reason.

That habit builds trust, and it keeps our phone number on refrigerators around Natick.

Our promise is simple. We answer the phone, tell you what we see and give you the price before we start. If you ever feel a plumber is pushing you toward work you do not need, ask for a second opinion, including ours.

What we will not do

We will not quote a price we cannot stand behind, promise an arrival time we cannot keep or push a replacement when a repair is the better answer. If a fixture can be saved, we say so. If it cannot, we show you why.
