Cold water only? Check these before we arrive

A few quick checks can save a service call or at least speed it up. For an electric heater, look at the breaker and press the reset button on the upper thermostat. For gas, see whether the pilot is lit and whether the gas valve is open. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.

If the tank is leaking, shut the cold supply and the fuel as described on our water heater replacement page. We take repair calls overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available.

Gas or electric: what changes in the repair

Gas heaters add a burner, a pilot or igniter and a vent to the list of parts that can fail. We check combustion air and flue draft because a poor vent can send exhaust back into the room. Electric heaters swap those for heating elements and thermostats, which are simpler but still need power safely isolated.

Either way, we test the heater under load before we leave. If the part is cheap and the tank is sound, repair is the better call. If the tank body is leaking, we say so and offer a price for installing a new unit.

Why Natick tanks collect sediment

Natick's 2025 water report puts hardness at about 108 parts per million. Hard water drops minerals as it heats, and those minerals settle at the bottom of the tank. Over time they insulate the water from the burner or elements, so the heater works harder and makes noise.

A yearly flush slows that down. It is a simple job when the drain valve still works. If it is seized shut or the sediment is rock-hard, we tell you plainly, because forcing it can cause a leak.

What a repair visit includes

We do more than swap the one part you called about.

  • A check of the temperature setting and the relief valve.
  • A look at the anode rod condition when access allows, since a spent rod speeds up corrosion, which our replacement page explains.
  • A leak check at the fittings, shutoffs and flue connection.
  • A plain explanation of what failed and what to watch next.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Repairs on gas appliances are covered by the state's gas fitting licensing, and relief valve and venting corrections follow 248 CMR and the manufacturer's guidelines. If a repair changes the vent or the fuel line, ask the Natick building department about permit requirements.
