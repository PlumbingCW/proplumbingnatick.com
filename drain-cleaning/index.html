Drain backing up? Stop the water first

Stop running water anywhere in the house, including the dishwasher and washing machine. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and a stuck drain sends it back up through the lowest opening. That is often a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub.

While you wait, skip the chemical cleaners. They can sit in the pipe, burn skin and make our job less safe. Put towels around any overflow and call. We take drain emergencies overnight and on weekends, with same-day appointments when available.

Which drain is it? Each one points to a different cause

Where the water backs up tells us a lot before we arrive.

One sink or tub.

A local clog in the trap or the branch line. Hair, soap scum and grease are the usual causes.

Several fixtures at once.

A blockage in the main drain or sewer lateral, not in a single fixture.

Basement floor drain.

The main line is likely full. Stop all water use and call right away.

Gurgling toilets.

Air is struggling to pass a partial clog or a blocked vent.

Slow kitchen sink.

Grease and food solids narrowing the line, often past the trap.

Recurring clogs.

Roots, a sag or scale in older pipe. Cleaning helps, but a camera look is wiser.

Snaking or jetting: which one fits your clog

We pick the method after hearing the symptoms, and we explain why.

Drain snake (cable machine)

  • Punches through a blockage and restores flow.
  • Good for hair, paper and a single stubborn clog.
  • Faster and lower cost on simple jobs.
  • May leave grease on the pipe wall.

Hydro jetting.

  • Scours the whole pipe wall with high-pressure water.
  • Better for grease, scale and root hairs.
  • Costs more, so it fits recurring problems.
  • See our hydro jetting service for details.

When the clog is bigger than one drain

If more than one fixture backs up, the blockage may be in the main line that leaves your house. In Natick, sewer charges are billed from your water meter, and the town sewer is treated by the MWRA, so the line from your foundation to the street is your responsibility to keep clear. Tree roots and old clay joints are frequent causes.

A main line clog is a different job from a sink clog. If that is what we find, we will tell you and explain whether sewer line cleaning or a camera inspection is the next step.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

Cleaning a drain follows industry standards for equipment and does not usually involve code changes, but opening or adding a cleanout, trap or vent does. Those changes follow 248 CMR, the Massachusetts plumbing code. If a plumber proposes altering your drain layout, ask whether a permit applies and who pulls it.

Sources: 248 CMR and equipment maker guidelines for cable and jetting tools.
