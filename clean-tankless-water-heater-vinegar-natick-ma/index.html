How to Clean a Tankless Water Heater With Vinegar in Natick

Scale shortens the life of a tankless heater. A yearly vinegar flush is a common fix, and here is how it works and where it can go wrong.

Why tankless units scale up

A tankless heater heats water as it passes through a heat exchanger. Minerals in the water leave a thin crust on the hot surfaces, the same way a kettle scales. Over time the crust makes the unit work harder, reduces flow and can trigger error codes.

The fix is to dissolve the scale with an acid, and white vinegar is the common household choice. It is mild and food safe. It also takes time, so a flush is a project, not a quick rinse.

How hard is Natick water

Natick's 2025 water quality report lists hardness at about 108 parts per million, ranging from 81.4 to 134. One grain per gallon equals 17.1 parts per million, so Natick's average is about 6.3 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard. It will scale a tankless unit over a year or two without maintenance.

The report also lists chlorine between 0.17 and 1.0 parts per million. For your heater, the hardness is what matters. A yearly flush is a reasonable habit in Natick, and sooner if you notice reduced flow or error codes.

Source: Town of Natick 2025 Consumer Confidence Report.

What you need

Gather these before you start.

  • Your heater's manual, because it may specify the descaling method.
  • A small submersible pump with hoses, a bucket and fittings that connect to the service valves.
  • About three to four gallons of white vinegar, or the amount your manual calls for.
  • Garden hose and a place to drain.
  • Gloves, eye protection and towels.

The flush, step by step

Always follow your manufacturer's instructions first. This is the general approach.

1

Power down and shut off.

Turn off the unit, the gas and the water supply.

2

Connect hoses to the service valves.

Open the service ports and connect the pump hoses to the hot and cold sides.

3

Circulate vinegar.

Fill the bucket with vinegar and run the pump through the unit for about an hour, as many manufacturers suggest.

4

Drain and rinse.

Drain the vinegar and flush with clean water.

5

Clean the inlet filter.

Remove the screen, rinse it and put it back.

6

Restore and test.

Reopen the valves, power the unit and check for leaks and error codes.

Mistakes that damage units

The common ones are avoidable.

Skipping the manual

According to many manufacturer guidelines, the descaling solution and flow rate are specified by model. Ignoring it can void a warranty.

Using harsh acids

Stronger acids can damage seals and the heat exchanger. White vinegar is gentle for a reason.

Forgetting to restore the isolation valves

Leaving a service valve in the wrong position can starve the unit of water and make it shut off or overheat.

Not cleaning the inlet screen

The screen traps debris. A clogged screen mimics scale and fools many owners.

How often to flush

In moderately hard water, once a year is a common rule. If your household uses a lot of hot water, or you see a drop in flow, sooner is better. A water softener changes the picture, and we cover it below. Keep a log with the date so you do not have to guess.

If you are deciding between a tank and a tankless unit, see our tankless water heater page. If your unit is throwing codes, our repair team can diagnose it.

Reading the signs of scale before the flush

Scale rarely announces itself. The first sign is usually a hot water supply that feels weaker than it used to, or a unit that cycles on and off. Some models show an error code for low flow or high exhaust temperature as the heat exchanger loses efficiency. If you see a code, check your manual before you assume a part has failed, because the cause is often a simple flush.

Noise is another clue. A kettle-like rumbling or ticking inside the unit means mineral deposits are trapping small pockets of steam against the metal. That wears the exchanger faster. A flush ends the noise and protects the unit.

Do you need to clean the filter and the vent too

Yes, and many owners forget. The cold water inlet screen catches grit and flakes, and it clogs long before the exchanger fouls. Rinse it each time you flush. The vent and the intake air path matter too. Birds, leaves and lint can block them, and a blocked vent can shut a gas unit down for safety. A quick look outside at the vent termination takes a minute.

If your tankless unit is not installed with service valves, ask a plumber to add them. The small cost pays for itself the first time you flush the unit without cutting a pipe.

Signs the flush worked

After a flush, hot water should arrive faster and stay steadier. Error codes related to flow or exhaust temperature should clear. The unit should run quieter. If symptoms return within weeks, there may be a different cause, such as a failing sensor, a gas supply problem or a venting issue. At that point, a service visit is the right next step.

Softeners and scale filters

A softener or scale-inhibiting filter reduces how fast scale forms. They are not required, but they help if your water is on the harder side of what Natick delivers. Ask your plumber before you buy so the unit is sized correctly and does not hurt your flow.

In Ashland, the town's report did not list hardness, so ask the Department of Public Works before choosing equipment. In Needham, the water is on the soft side by comparison.
