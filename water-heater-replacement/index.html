Water heater failed? Do these three things first

If water is pooling around the tank, close the cold water valve on the pipe above the heater. Then cut the fuel. For an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Those three moves stop the flooding and keep the heater from firing into an empty tank.

While you wait for us, keep a bucket under any drip and move stored boxes off the floor. Do not open the drain valve on a hot tank, since the water inside can scald. We take replacement calls overnight and on weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

Signs a replacement beats another repair

Repairs make sense for a bad element or thermostat. These signs usually point to the tank itself giving out.

  • Water at the base of the tank. A leak from the tank body, as opposed to a fitting, means the steel lining has failed and no part will fix it.
  • Rust-colored hot water. Rust only in the hot taps points to corrosion inside the tank, not in your house pipes.
  • Rumbling or popping. Hardened sediment on the bottom traps steam bubbles. Heavy buildup shortens the life of the tank and wastes fuel.
  • Age past ten to twelve years. Tanks installed in the 2000s are often at the end of their run, especially if the anode rod was never checked.
  • Repeat repairs. A second service call in a single year is a good moment to price a new unit against another patch.

Tank or tankless for a Natick home

Most replacements stay with the same type, but a failure is also the cheapest time to switch. Here is how the two compare.

Conventional tank

  • Lower purchase price and simple to service.
  • Stores hot water, so it can run out during back-to-back showers.
  • Fits existing gas lines and flues in most Natick basements.
  • Gets a swap in a single visit more often than not.

Tankless.

  • Heats water on demand and frees floor space.
  • Often needs a larger gas line, new venting or added electrical capacity.
  • Needs yearly flushing, which matters with moderately hard water.
  • Higher upfront cost, so read our tankless page before deciding.

What Natick water does to your tank

Natick's 2025 water quality report lists hardness around 108 parts per million, about 6.3 grains per gallon. That is moderate, but over a decade it still leaves a layer of scale and sediment on the bottom of a tank. Homes near Lake Cochituate and out toward Walnut Hill often show this on the first drain test.

The practical takeaway is simple. A tank that was never flushed or had its anode rod checked will fail sooner. If your old unit is under fifteen years and still leaked, we will tell you why, which helps the next one last longer.

What replacement day looks like

The visit is short when the space is clear and the new unit is chosen ahead of time.

1

Shut down and drain.

We close the water and fuel, then drain the old tank safely to a floor drain or outdoors.

2

Remove the old unit.

The old heater comes out and leaves with us, so you are not stuck with a rusty tank in the garage.

3

Set and connect.

The new heater is set level, then supply lines, shutoffs and the relief valve are connected to current standards.

4

Vent, fuel and power.

Gas units get a vent and leak check. Electric units get proper wiring checks before power is restored.

5

Test and explain.

We run the unit through a heating cycle, check for leaks and show you the shutoff, the temperature setting and what to watch.

What decides the price of a replacement

No two replacements cost the same, and we would rather say that than guess. Here is what moves the number.

Unit type and size. A forty-gallon tank and a whole-house tankless unit sit in different price bands.
Fuel and venting. Switching fuel or rerouting a flue adds parts and labor.
Location. A cramped attic or a finished basement with tight access takes longer than an open utility room.
Code updates. Older installs often need an expansion tank, new shutoffs or a corrected relief line.
Permit steps. Ask the Natick building department whether your job needs a permit. We can tell you what the work involves.
Hear the number before the old tank moves

Insurance usually helps only with sudden water damage from a burst tank, not with a worn-out heater. Tell us the brand, the fuel and where the heater sits, and the price you hear on the phone is the one we work from.

Savings worth checking before you buy

Mass Save lists heat pump water heaters among its related rebates and incentives. Amounts and rules change, so check the current program page or ask your utility before you order. If you want one, tell us on the call so we can plan the electrical and space needs. For a replacement that is not an emergency, our water heater installation team can schedule it around your calendar.

Code, permits and licensing for this work

A replacement has to meet the state plumbing code, 248 CMR, and the manufacturer's installation standards for the specific unit. Those instructions set clearances, relief valve routing and venting. Gas work falls under the gas fitting rules the state board licenses. Ask the Natick building department whether your swap needs a permit.
