What Happens on Water Heater Install Day in Natick, MA

Install day goes smoothly when the space is ready and you know the order of work. Here is how it usually runs in a Natick house.

Install-day guide

Published 2026-10-11. Updated 2026-11-14.

Before the plumber arrives

The best thing you can do is clear the path. Move boxes, bikes and laundry baskets away from the water heater and from the stairs the old tank will go up. Know where the main water valve is. If the heater is gas, make sure the plumber can reach the gas shutoff. If it is electric, know which breaker feeds it.

Plan to be home for the start and the finish. Hot water will be off for a few hours, so shower before the visit. If you have not yet chosen a unit, read our water heater installation page to see how sizing works.

The order of the day

Every install follows the same arc, with small variations.

1

Walkthrough and price check.

The plumber looks at the space, confirms the unit and repeats the price. Changes are discussed before any tool is picked up.

2

Shut down.

Water, fuel and power are turned off. The old tank is drained to a floor drain or a hose.

3

Disconnect and remove.

Pipes, vent and fuel lines come off. The old tank goes out the door.

4

Set and connect.

The new heater is placed level on a stable base. Supply lines, shutoffs and the relief valve are connected.

5

Fuel, vent and power.

Gas units get a leak check and vent inspection. Electric units get wiring checks.

6

Fill, purge and test.

The tank is filled, air is bled from the lines and the unit is fired up and tested through a heating cycle.

Parts that are often replaced during the install

Some items are cheap to replace now and annoying to replace later.

PartWhy it is replacedWhat to expect
Shutoff valves.Old valves often do not close fully.New ball valves on the supply.
Flexible connectors.Older connectors can corrode.New braided stainless connectors.
Relief valve and discharge pipe.A new unit should have a proper valve and a safe discharge route.New valve and a pipe that ends near the floor.
Expansion tank.A closed system needs room for expanding water.Added where the system calls for it.

Your plumber will tell you which of these apply to your system.

What Natick water means for the new tank

Natick draws its water from twelve groundwater wells at five locations, ten in Natick and two in Dover, and treats it at four facilities, according to the town's 2025 water quality report. Hardness averaged about 108 parts per million, about 6.3 grains per gallon. That is moderate water, so a tank will build sediment slowly.

The practical step is to flush the tank once a year and to have the anode rod checked every few years. A few minutes of maintenance each year can add real life to the new tank, and it keeps the warranty paperwork in order.

Source: Town of Natick 2025 Consumer Confidence Report.

Before the plumber leaves, check these

Walk through the checklist together.

  • The water is hot at the nearest tap within a few minutes.
  • No drips at the shutoffs, connections or relief valve.
  • The relief valve discharge pipe is in place and pointed at the floor.
  • The temperature setting is where you want it, commonly in the range many manufacturers suggest.
  • You know where the shutoff, the breaker or the gas valve is.
  • You have the unit's manual and registration details.

Small things that cause big delays

A few surprises slow installs down.

Unexpected venting problems

Older Natick basements sometimes have a vent that no longer meets current practice. Fixing it takes time and parts, and we explain the change before we start.

Seized fittings

Old threaded fittings can break when forced. A plumber has the tools to deal with it, but it can add time.

Tight access

Narrow stairs or a cramped closet make carrying and setting the tank harder. Measure the path before the day.

Missing permits or paperwork

Ask the Natick building department whether your job needs a permit, and tell the plumber so the schedule includes it.

Questions to ask about the warranty

Tanks come with a limited warranty on the tank and a shorter one on parts. Ask what is covered, how long it lasts and what the manufacturer expects from you in maintenance. Keep the paperwork where you will find it. Our water heater replacement page explains what to do if the old unit fails before the install.

If you are in Framingham or Wellesley, install day looks the same. The difference is in the water, which we cover on the town pages.

Gas, electric and the parts that surprise people

Gas heaters add a vent and a burner to the list of things that can go wrong on install day. A flue that has been slowly corroding for years is easy to miss until the old unit comes out. If the plumber finds one, replacing a section is the safe answer, and it is better to hear it at nine in the morning than at three in the afternoon.

Electric heaters are simpler but still depend on the wiring. A circuit that has been warm for years or a breaker that trips can mean a bigger electrical job. We stop and explain when that happens, since wiring is a separate trade.

Ten minutes of prep that save an hour

Photograph the old heater's label, which lists the model, capacity and fuel. Clear a three-foot working space and put down a drop cloth if you have a finished floor. Turn the temperature of any other appliance that depends on hot water, like a dishwasher, to off for the day so nothing runs dry.

Tell everyone in the house the water will be off. A plumber finishing a connection should not be surprised by a sudden flush upstairs.

After the install

Check the area around the tank over the next few days. A small drip from a connection is common as materials settle, and it is easy to tighten. If you smell gas or see water pooling, shut off the unit and call. Run a hot tap in the farthest bathroom and note how long it takes for hot water to arrive. That gives you a baseline to compare against later.

Mark a calendar reminder for a year from now. A quick flush and a pressure check keep problems small. If you are curious about repair rather than replacement, see our water heater repair page.
